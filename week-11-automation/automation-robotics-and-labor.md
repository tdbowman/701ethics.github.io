# Week 11: Automation, Robotics & Labor ⚙️

**Block 4: Emerging Technologies & Future Ethics | November 2 – November 8, 2026**

---

*Every automation decision is a labor decision, whether or not anyone says so out loud.*

Last week asked whether an algorithm can be fair. This week asks who gets paid, and it is harder to hold steadily because the honest answers are unflattering in both directions.

The first difficulty: almost every argument for automation in libraries is an argument about access. Machines describe collections human catalogers will never reach; automated storage moves material faster; a chatbot answers at 2 a.m. If access is a core professional value, you cannot treat automation as simply a threat without explaining to the patron who cannot find the thing why your job security outranks their access. Padilla puts it positively. Libraries want algorithmic methods for "creating efficiencies in collection description, discovery, and access; freeing up staff time to meet evolving demands; and improving the overall library user experience." Those are good reasons.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Read Padilla on responsible operations, and on constructive dissent as a professional duty
- Ask what robots in libraries actually do, and whose work they displace or absorb
- Separate automation that extends professional judgment from automation that replaces it
- Trace training-data provenance and rights assessment when the scale defeats manual review

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- Padilla argues that responsible operations require "constructive dissent" with institutional protection for objectors. What would that protection consist of for a new hire on a one-year contract?
- Oladokun et al. find robotics preparedness varies with funding and infrastructure. Does a literature that theorizes automation from well-resourced institutions produce guidance that is useless, or actively harmful, elsewhere?
- If AI genuinely expands access to collections human labor could never describe, on what grounds does labor solidarity outrank access? Answer without retreating to "both matter."
