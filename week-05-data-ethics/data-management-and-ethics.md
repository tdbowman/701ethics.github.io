# Week 5: Data Management & Ethics 🗄️

**Block 2: Privacy, Data Ethics & Digital Equity | September 21 – 27, 2026**

---

*Learning analytics promises to show you who needs help. Read what it costs to find out.*

Week 4 was about protecting patrons from outsiders: the officer at the desk, the platform, the breach. This week turns the lens around: what do libraries and librarians do with patron data themselves, in good faith, for defensible institutional reasons, and is "we needed it for assessment" a sufficient answer?

That reframing is why the Fall 2026 list replaces Lyon's 2016 transparency article with Salo's 2024 study. Lyon argued that libraries should become "transparency agents" in the open-science ecosystem, and that research data services, reproducibility infrastructure, and data-librarian roles position libraries as guarantors of research integrity. Generous, and still worth reading. But it treats the library as the party enforcing research ethics on others. Salo asks what happens when the library is the researcher and the patron is the subject, and finds that the profession's own literature does not survive the standard Lyon wanted libraries to hold others to.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Apply the NISO principles to a vendor relationship you actually have
- Work through Salo on library learning analytics and the re-identification trifecta
- Treat the SPARC vendor analysis as a method you can repeat on your own systems
- Weigh transparency against information hazard, without pretending the trade-off is free

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- Does a library's need to demonstrate its value to administrators generate a legitimate claim on patron data, and if so, what limits that claim?
- If neither IRBs nor journal editors examine library-specific privacy risk, who does, and what would it take to build that capacity?
- When a vendor's platform permits hundreds of third parties to collect data from your patrons, what is the library's realistic recourse: contract language, consortial pressure, migration, or refusal?
