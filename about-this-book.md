# About This Book 📘

This open online textbook was created for **LIS 701: Core Values, Ethics, and Issues in the Library and Information Profession** at Dominican University's School of Information Studies, for the Fall 2026 offering.

---

## Why an Open Textbook?

1. **Cost**: You pay nothing. Every assigned reading is openly licensed, freely available, or provided through Perusall and Canvas. A course whose readings argue for information access should not put its own textbook behind a paywall.
2. **Currency**: This field moves. Between the 2025 and 2026 editions of this course, the ALA adopted new AI guidance, ACRL published AI competencies, three interpretations of the Library Bill of Rights were amended, and a federal executive order put library funding in question. A printed textbook could not have kept up.
3. **Honesty about sources**: Every chapter tells you where its claims come from and links you to the original. You are never more than one click from the primary source, which is the habit this course is trying to build in you.

## How It Was Built

The book is written in [MyST Markdown](https://mystmd.org/) and built with [Jupyter Book](https://jupyterbook.org). The source lives on [GitHub](https://github.com/tdbowman/701ethics.github.io) and the site rebuilds automatically whenever the source changes.

Summaries were written from the source texts themselves wherever the text could be obtained, and every citation was checked against a published record. Where a reading could not be verified, the entry says so on its face. The full account is on the [Editorial Notes](editorial-notes.md) page, including a list of citation errors this rebuild found and corrected.

Portions of the drafting were assisted by AI tools, with all content reviewed, curated, and approved by Dr. Bowman. That disclosure is not a formality. In a course that spends three weeks on algorithmic ethics and ends with an essay about arguing with an algorithm, hiding the workflow would be indefensible.

## A Living Document

If you spot an error, a dead link, or a summary that misreads its source, tell Dr. Bowman. The text always wins over the summary, and catching the difference is a professional skill.

## Relationship to Canvas and Perusall

This book is your **content home**: what to read, what it argues, and what to think about. **Perusall** is where you annotate and where your reading grade comes from. **Canvas** is your **logistics home**: submission links, exact due dates, rubrics, and grades. When the three disagree about logistics, Canvas wins.
