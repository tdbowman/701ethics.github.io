# Capstone: Arguing with the Algorithm ✍️

**Block 5: Synthesis & Professional Integration | December 6 (capstone report + synthesis form) · December 12 (final essay)**

---

*An algorithm is about to tell you what kind of information professional you are. Answer it.*

Here is your situation. You will upload the JSON exports from your Block 1–4 scenario performances to the integrator at https://sites.google.com/view/lis-701-block-5/home. It will return a composite score, dimension scores, and a philosophy label. Then you will write 1,800–2,400 words about it, worth 15% of your grade and the only instructor-graded item in the course. This is the one place where a human reads your reasoning and responds to it.

Start with what the report is, because the instructor has told you and it matters. The scores are generated from his own reading of the course materials, and they are explicitly *not* an official assessment metric; they exist to give you something to think with. That single fact resolves the most common way this essay goes wrong. Students who treat the report as a verdict write defensively — accepting it gratefully or rejecting it as unfair — and both produce a thin paper. The report is a *model*, built by a particular person with particular readings in mind, applied to choices you made inside constructed scenarios. Models can be characterized, tested, and argued with. Verdicts can only be accepted or appealed.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Read a generated report about yourself as an argument with premises, not a measurement
- Choose four readings that give you leverage rather than decoration
- Move from critique to commitment without collapsing into either cynicism or compliance
- Name one tension you have not resolved, and say why it is still open

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- What construct does each dimension score claim to measure, what did the integrator actually observe, and how wide is the gap?
- The instructor states the scores are not an official assessment metric and derive from his own reading of the course materials. Does that disclosure resolve the explicability problem, mitigate it, or relocate it?
- Which recorded choices do you still endorse, which do you now regard as wrong, and what changed — a reading, an argument, or an incentive?
- What are you committing to that you can actually be held to, by whom, and on what timeline?
- What is the tension in your professional position that you have not resolved, and what would you need in order to resolve it?
