## 🗝️ Key Concepts

| Term | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Learning ecosystem** | Varlejs's frame for the network of colleagues, employers, associations, vendors, universities, and government agencies, each of which can enable or discourage a practitioner's learning (IFLA CPD Guidelines, Introduction). |
| **Personal learning plan** | a written statement of what you need to learn, why, how, by when, and how you will know you have learned it (IFLA CPD Guidelines, §1.2.4). |
| **Competency gap** | the distance between the competencies your role requires and those you hold, identified through self-assessment against published competency indexes and through performance appraisal (§1.2.3). |
| **Transfer of training** | whether learning from an event actually changes workplace practice; more likely when the learner is prepared, the design promotes transfer, and the workplace supports application (§5.2.6). |
| **Kirkpatrick's four levels** | reaction, learning, behavior, results; the evaluation sequence the Guidelines adopt, noting most providers reach only the first two (§5.2.7). |
| **AI system life cycle** | UNESCO's organizing unit, from research and design through trade, monitoring, end-of-use, and termination; obligations attach to stages, not technologies (UNESCO, §I.2(b)). |
| **Ethical impact assessment (EIA)** | UNESCO's Policy Area 1: a transparent, multidisciplinary assessment of an AI system's benefits, risks, and human-rights impacts before deployment (paras. 50–53). |
| **Human oversight and determination** | the principle that ethical and legal responsibility must always attach to natural or legal persons, and that AI systems must not be given legal personality (paras. 35–36, 68). |
| **Awareness and literacy** | UNESCO's principle that public understanding of AI and of the value of data must be actively cultivated so people can make informed decisions and resist undue influence (para. 44). |
| **LSTA Grants to States** | the formula-based federal funding stream, administered by IMLS through state library agencies, whose interruption the ALA FAQ describes. |

All terms from all sixteen weeks are collected in the [Glossary](../glossary.md).

## 💬 Annotation & Discussion Prompts

These are written for **Perusall**. Each one points at something specific and each one is arguable, which is the point: annotation credit comes from advancing a conversation, not from summarizing a paragraph.

1. Varlejs writes that "few professionals perceive themselves as lacking proficiency in tasks they perform regularly" (§1.2.1). Annotate that passage with the competency you are *most* confident about, and argue either that your confidence is warranted or that this sentence is about you.
2. The Guidelines recommend 0.5%–1.0% of institutional budget for staff development, then call that figure "truly minimal" (§2.2.5). Is publishing a number you consider inadequate a useful advocacy move or a self-defeating one?
3. UNESCO paragraph 26 states that "AI systems should not be used for social scoring or mass surveillance purposes." Argue whether a library's algorithmic assessment of a student's ethical reasoning is a form of social scoring. You may conclude that the Block 5 integrator fails this test.
4. Paragraph 11 concedes that UNESCO's values and principles will conflict. Annotate it with one concrete conflict between two named principles that could arise in a public library, and say which should give way.
5. The ALA FAQ ends with a call to contact Congress. Does asking members to lobby compromise the association's authority as an ethical standards body, or is silence the greater compromise? Use one Block 1 reading.
6. The 2025 Part 2 proposes AI-driven personalization of professional learning. Argue whether letting a system infer your learning needs differs meaningfully from letting a system infer your ethical profile.

## 🔗 Connections

- **Scenario:** The capstone will hand you a composite score, dimension scores, and a philosophy label. The IFLA Guidelines give you the frame for receiving that: it is a self-assessment instrument, and Varlejs has already said self-assessment "is subjective and does not come with feedback" and depends on the user's ability to recognize gaps (§1.2.1). Before you upload your JSON files, decide what you would count as a *useful* result — one that would actually change what you do next — and write it down. That prediction is evidence in your essay.
- **Final essay:** Week 13 supplies the leadership frame for Part 2. Your commitments should read like entries in a personal learning plan — specific, with a named competency gap, a named learning resource, and a way you would know you had closed it. UNESCO's human oversight principle gives you the strongest single lever for Part 1: if an AI system can never replace ultimate human responsibility and accountability, then a report assigning you a philosophy label has not thereby determined your philosophy, and you can say so with a citation behind you.
- **Threads back:** This closes the loop on Block 1. The professional philosophy you drafted there was a statement of belief with no infrastructure attached. Week 13 asks what it would take — in hours, dollars, associations, and political advocacy — to still hold it in a decade.
