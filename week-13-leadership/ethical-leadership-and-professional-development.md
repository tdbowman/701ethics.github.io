# Week 13: Ethical Leadership & Professional Development 🧭

**Block 5: Synthesis & Professional Integration | November 16 – 22, 2026**

---

*Your license to keep practicing is your own continuing education. Also, the funding is not safe.*

You have spent twelve weeks learning to reason about hard cases. This week the question changes shape. It is no longer "what is the right thing to do here?" but "what kind of practitioner will still be able to answer that in fifteen years, and what does the institution around you owe you so that you can?" That is what professional development actually is, once you strip away the language of workshops and webinars. It is the infrastructure of continued moral competence.

The three texts sit at three altitudes, and the friction between them is the point. The IFLA Guidelines for Continuing Professional Development work at the level of the individual and the employer: who is responsible for keeping a librarian's expertise current, how much budget and how many working hours that takes, and how you would know whether any of it worked. The UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence works at the level of the state, a standard-setting instrument adopted by all UNESCO Member States, with values, principles, and eleven areas of policy action. The ALA FAQ on the executive order targeting the Institute of Museum and Library Services works at the level of the funded present: what happens to professional development, to talking books, to interlibrary loan, when the federal line item that pays for them is targeted for elimination.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Build a personal learning plan you would actually follow after this course ends
- Read UNESCO's Recommendation for its machinery, especially the ethical impact assessment
- Understand human oversight and determination as a policy requirement, not a slogan
- See what the IMLS executive order does to the funding your work depends on

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- If the individual practitioner is "primarily responsible" for ongoing learning, what does the employing institution still owe you, and what follows if it does not deliver?
- UNESCO asks Member States to require ethical impact assessments of AI systems. What would it take for a library to actually perform one, and who there would have the competence to do it?
- When federal funding for libraries becomes a live political question, is advocacy a professional duty or a personal choice? And what does your answer imply about the neutrality claims you examined in Block 1?
