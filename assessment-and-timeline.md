## The Components, Explained

### 1. Immersive Scenario Performances (55%)

Five scenarios, one per block. Each puts you in a role and makes you decide under pressure, then scores the pattern of your choices.

| Block | Scenario | Due |
|-------|----------|-----|
| 1 | **The Censorship Crisis**: you are a new director facing organized pressure over a title | Sun, Sep 13 |
| 2 | **Data Ethics Simulation**: law enforcement requests, vendor negotiations, and a community that wants both privacy and convenience | Sun, Oct 4 |
| 3 | **Building Inclusive Spaces**: the same services, experienced from the perspectives of patrons your systems were not built for | Sun, Oct 25 |
| 4 | **AI Implementation Decision Tree**: deploying AI services, negotiating with a vendor, and pricing efficiency against your values | Sun, Nov 15 |
| 5 | **Ethics Journey Capstone**: your Block 1–4 exports, integrated into one report | Sun, Dec 6 |

- Graded complete/incomplete against your reflections, and spot-checked
- The **Block 2 simulation requires 75/100**; retries are unlimited, so retry it
- Export your JSON report every time. Block 5 needs all of them

### 2. Reading Engagement in Perusall (20%)

Auto-scored annotation, every week. The end of each week in this book gives you prompts written to earn this credit honestly: point at a passage, take a position, respond to a classmate.

### 3. Ethics Journey Capstone (10%)

Upload your scenario exports to the [Block 5 integrator](https://sites.google.com/view/lis-701-block-5/home), which generates a composite report with dimension scores and a philosophy label, then complete the synthesis form.

:::{note} These scores are not an assessment metric
The numbers come from Dr. Bowman's own reading of the course materials. They exist to give you something concrete to argue with in the final essay. Upload your first, honest run rather than an optimized one; a defensive performance makes for a boring essay.
:::

### 4. Final Essay: *Arguing with the Algorithm* (15%)

6–8 pages, due Saturday, December 12, in two parts:

- **Part 1**: critique the algorithmically generated report about your own performance, using **at least one Block 4 reading**. What did it measure? What did it mistake a proxy for? Where is it right in a way you did not want to hear?
- **Part 2**: your professional philosophy, with **2–3 actionable commitments** and **one unresolved tension** you are still carrying.

Requirements: at least **four readings** from at least **two blocks**, including Block 4. APA 7. Rubric weighted 30/20/30/20. The [Capstone chapter](capstone/arguing-with-the-algorithm.md) walks you through it.

## A Suggested Weekly Routine

1. **Early week**: read the week's pages here, then the assigned texts
2. **Midweek**: annotate in Perusall, and respond to at least one classmate
3. **End of block**: run the scenario in one sitting, then write the reflections while it is fresh
4. **From Week 10 on**: keep a running file of essay material. Six to eight pages is much easier to build across six weeks than across one weekend
