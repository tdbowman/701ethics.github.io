This open textbook is your companion for one uncomfortable question: **what do you owe the people who use the systems you build and run?** A parent challenges a book. A detective asks for circulation records. A vendor offers personalization in exchange for patron data. A cataloging rule quietly decides who counts as normal. An algorithm sorts your patrons, and then it sorts you. None of these has a clean answer, which is exactly why the profession wrote codes about them and exactly why the codes are not enough.

## The Course in One Picture

The semester runs on a single loop:

- **You read the field's own arguments**: manifestos, codes, empirical studies, and critiques that disagree with each other in public
- **You walk into a scenario and decide**: five immersive simulations put you in the chair where the decision is actually made
- **You are scored, and then you argue back**: the capstone generates a report about your ethical reasoning, and your final essay tells it where it is wrong

## What You'll Wrestle With

- 🏛️ **When your community and your professional code disagree, which one loses?**
- 🔐 **Is a library that cannot protect patron data from its own vendors still protecting privacy?**
- 🤝 **Can a profession that calls itself neutral serve people its systems were not built for?**
- 🤖 **When an algorithm makes a judgment about a person, what standing do you have to overrule it?**
- 🧭 **What will you actually do differently, on a Tuesday, because of what you believe?**

## How This Book Works

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| 📖 **A reader's companion** | Every week tells you what each assigned text argues and why it is there. The book is the map; the readings are the territory. |
| 🗣️ **Built for Perusall** | Each week ends in annotation prompts that point at specific passages and take a position worth arguing with. |
| 🎮 **Aimed at the scenarios** | Every week connects explicitly to the block's immersive simulation and to the final essay. |
| ⚠️ **Honest about itself** | Where a citation could not be verified, the book says so on the page rather than bluffing. |
