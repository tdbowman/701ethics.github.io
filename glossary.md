# Glossary 🗝️

Every key term from the sixteen weeks, 161 in all, drawn from the readings themselves. The **Week** column links back to where the term is introduced.

---


## A

| Term | Definition | Week |
|------|------------|------|
| **Actionable commitment** | a statement specific enough that someone else could tell whether you kept it; it names a behavior, a context, and a way of knowing. "Advocate for privacy" is not one; "in any vendor evaluation I participate in, ask in writing what patron data the product retains and for how long, and record the answer" is. | [Capstone](capstone/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Adoption vs. access** | Grimes and Porter's distinction from the New Jersey commission's work: availability of a broadband line is not the same as a household taking it up, and subsidy programs address the second only partially because of mobility, eviction, and payment interruption. | [Week 6](week-06-digital-divide/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **AI literacy predicated on use** | the structural feature Baer identifies in the Barnard, Stanford, and ACRL frameworks, in which understanding and evaluation follow use, leaving non-use unavailable as a literate position (Baer 2025). | [Week 16](week-16-futures/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **AI refusal** | the range of ways individuals or groups consciously and intentionally decline generative AI use where able; explicitly not monolithic, not ignorance, and not equivalent to banning student use (Baer 2025, citing Sano-Franchini, McIntyre, & Fernandes 2024). | [Week 16](week-16-futures/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **AI system life cycle** | UNESCO's organizing unit, from research and design through trade, monitoring, end-of-use, and termination; obligations attach to stages, not technologies (UNESCO, §I.2(b)). | [Week 13](week-13-leadership/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **AI-readiness** | the condition in which a collection's documentation, metadata, provenance, and formats suffice for computational use; a property of the collection, not the software (Colavizza & Jaillant, 2026). | [Week 15](week-15-preservation/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **AI-ready collections** | IFLA's framing of holdings prepared and licensed for computational use; the copyright-side counterpart to Padilla's machine-actionable collections. | [Week 12](week-12-makerspaces/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Algorithmic bias** | biased outcomes arising from the algorithm or from historic data reflecting past inequalities, which AI reproduces (Cox, p. 205); ARL's Principle 2 frames it as "distortions" inherent to all AI. | [Week 10](week-10-ai-ethics/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Anonymization vs. deidentification** | NISO Principle 5 and Salo both treat full anonymization of rich data as effectively unattainable; deidentification reduces risk without guaranteeing safety, and hashing an identifier is not deidentification. | [Week 5](week-05-data-ethics/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Arrival technology** | Enis's framing of generative AI as already-present and society-reshaping; a move worth naming, because it converts a contestable adoption decision into an adaptation problem. | [Week 11](week-11-automation/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Article 19 (UDHR)** | The right to seek, receive, and impart information regardless of frontiers, cited by both the IFLA Code and Knox as grounding intellectual freedom as a human right rather than a professional preference. | [Week 3](week-03-codes/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Auditability** | NISO Principle 12's requirement of periodic independent review with results available to libraries on request; the mechanism the vendor reports test. | [Week 5](week-05-data-ethics/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Autonomy** | the balance between decision-making power humans retain and power delegated to artificial agents, the risk being that growth in artificial autonomy undermines human flourishing (§3.3). | [Week 14](week-14-global-citizenship/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Awareness and literacy** | UNESCO's principle that public understanding of AI and of the value of data must be actively cultivated so people can make informed decisions and resist undue influence (para. 44). | [Week 13](week-13-leadership/concepts-and-questions.md) |

## B

| Term | Definition | Week |
|------|------------|------|
| **Behavioral-exhaust data** | Data generated *about* rather than collected *from* patrons through ordinary system use; Salo's inclusion criterion and the raw material of library learning analytics. | [Week 5](week-05-data-ethics/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Belonging** | the felt experience of being genuinely welcomed; distinguished in the 2025 ALA interpretation from equity, diversity, and inclusion because it names a state in a person rather than an action by an institution. | [Week 7](week-07-edib/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Beneficence** | promoting well-being, preserving dignity, and sustaining the planet; the most widely shared of the five principles across the six documents (Floridi & Cowls, §3.1). | [Week 14](week-14-global-citizenship/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Big P / small p policy** | Formal law and regulation versus organizational guidelines and internal norms. Libraries mostly govern by small p but are increasingly constrained by big P. | [Week 2](week-02-intellectual-freedom/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Bit half-life** | the time after which there is a 50% probability a given bit has flipped; Rosenthal's proposed metric, because MTTDL relates to time but not bits and UBER relates to bits but not time (p. 50). | [Week 15](week-15-preservation/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Bit rot** | a process randomly flipping stored bits at a constant small probability per unit time; the residual failure mode remaining even if every correlated cause were eliminated (Rosenthal, p. 50). | [Week 15](week-15-preservation/concepts-and-questions.md) |

## C

| Term | Definition | Week |
|------|------------|------|
| **Canary metaphor** | Hanell et al.'s framing of public libraries as early indicators of democratic strain, registering political distress before other institutions (2025). | [Week 14](week-14-global-citizenship/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Capability over goods** | Britz following Sen: fair distribution of opportunities and capabilities matters more than fair distribution of goods, because only the former enables self-development. | [Week 9](week-09-global/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Challenge vs. ban** | ALA counts documented attempts to redact, restrict, relocate, or remove; PEN counts instances of removal or restricted access in schools. Different denominators, not additive. | [Week 2](week-02-intellectual-freedom/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Chilling effect** | Users who suspect monitoring alter what they seek; the harm that makes privacy instrumentally necessary to intellectual freedom (both ALA documents; Ocks & Salubi). | [Week 4](week-04-privacy/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Chilling effect** | the climate in which patrons do not feel safe searching for or reading what they want (Cox, p. 208). | [Week 10](week-10-ai-ethics/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Common-mode failure** | why replicating across identical systems is much weaker than replicating across diverse ones: one software bug damages the same data in every copy (p. 51). | [Week 15](week-15-preservation/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Communication chain of recorded information** | Creation, dissemination, collection, storage, organisation, description, retrieval, use, preservation. Bawden and Robinson's claim to uniqueness rests on caring about the whole chain. | [Week 1](week-01-disciplines/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Community anchor institution / failover mechanism** | Porter's framing of the library as the connection that persists regardless of housing status or ability to pay a subsidized bill; the argument for gigabit fiber as a library minimum. | [Week 6](week-06-digital-divide/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Competency gap** | the distance between the competencies your role requires and those you hold, identified through self-assessment against published competency indexes and through performance appraisal (§1.2.3). | [Week 13](week-13-leadership/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Connectivity, content, and human approaches** | Britz's three families of explanation: missing technology, missing or unaffordable content, and missing capacity to benefit. | [Week 9](week-09-global/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Construct validity** | whether an instrument measures what it claims to; the central question for any dimension score, because "ethical reasoning" is a construct and a branching-scenario choice is not the same thing. | [Capstone](capstone/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Constructive dissent** | a governance culture where staff are empowered to object and protected when they do; rules of ethical use are self-defeating without it (Padilla, p. 8). | [Week 11](week-11-automation/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Content-based vs. viewpoint-based restriction** | Chmara, quoted by Knox: content restrictions in an open forum survive only with a compelling interest and narrow tailoring; viewpoint restrictions "are impermissible in any form." | [Week 2](week-02-intellectual-freedom/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Contextual integrity** | Nissenbaum's alternative to notice-and-choice: privacy is preserved when flows conform to legitimate contextual norms, specified by subject, sender, recipient, information type, transmission principle (Gstrein & Beaulieu, §3.2.2). | [Week 4](week-04-privacy/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Contingency of categories** | Drabinski's core claim, via Foucault and Butler: identity categories are discursively produced and historically bound, so every correction is provisional and will itself become a target. | [Week 8](week-08-social-justice/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Copyright as a blunt instrument** | IFLA's warning that copyright should not be used to resolve ethical problems it was not designed for, which forces those problems back onto professional ethics. | [Week 12](week-12-makerspaces/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Counter-storytelling** | telling stories grounded in marginalized experience to contest dominant professional narratives; both a CRT method and, this block, a pedagogical form. | [Week 7](week-07-edib/concepts-and-questions.md) |

## D

| Term | Definition | Week |
|------|------------|------|
| **Data / information / knowledge** | Hepler and Horalek's ladder: bare facts, interpreted facts, internalized information. Useful as a teaching device, contested as a theory. | [Week 1](week-01-disciplines/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Data minimization, purpose limitation, storage limitation** | The three GDPR-derived principles adopted by name in the 2025 ALA interpretation: collect only what a disclosed purpose requires, use it only for that purpose, keep it only as long as needed. | [Week 4](week-04-privacy/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Data rescue** | grassroots and library-led efforts to capture and preserve federal data at risk of removal; named as a 2026 trend by ACRL and linked to the Data Rescue Project. | [Week 16](week-16-futures/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Data sovereignty** | ALA's insistence that patron data, institutional data, local history, staff workflows, and usage logs remain under library, public-sector, or community control. | [Week 10](week-10-ai-ethics/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Datafication** | Gstrein and Beaulieu's layered practice of rendering objects, relations, and interactions as data: community, forms of care, capacities, data — not reducible to any one technology. | [Week 4](week-04-privacy/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **De minimis contribution** | LCA's Principle 3 reasoning that any single work's contribution to an LLM is negligible, making per-work remuneration infeasible. | [Week 12](week-12-makerspaces/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Deresponsibilisation** | diffusion of accountability when a decision emerges from an opaque process rather than a human author (Cox, pp. 205–206). | [Week 10](week-10-ai-ethics/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Derivative data** | standardized, well-documented, consistently formatted versions created for computational use while originals are preserved unaltered (ARA guidelines, pillar 3). | [Week 15](week-15-preservation/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Designated public forum** | The status a library creates by opening meeting rooms; the 2019 Interpretation notes libraries need not offer them but incur forum obligations if they do. | [Week 2](week-02-intellectual-freedom/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Dialogic engagement** | Drabinski's alternative to correction: teaching users at the reference desk and in the classroom to read the catalog as a biased text rather than navigate it as a neutral one. | [Week 8](week-08-social-justice/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Digital equity** | NDIA: a *condition* in which all individuals and communities have the IT capacity needed for full participation in society, democracy, and economy. | [Week 6](week-06-digital-divide/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Digital inclusion** | NDIA: the *activities* required to reach that condition, comprising affordable broadband, adequate devices, literacy training, technical support, and enabling content. | [Week 6](week-06-digital-divide/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Digital inequality stack** | Robinson et al.'s layered model: inclusion requires access, use, consumption, literacies, skills, and production all working together; a gap in any layer produces exclusion regardless of the others. | [Week 6](week-06-digital-divide/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Digital navigator** | A trusted guide who assists community members with internet adoption and device use; operationally, a reference interview about technology need that addresses access, device, and skill in one encounter. | [Week 6](week-06-digital-divide/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Digital redlining** | The pattern by which infrastructure investment, pricing, and service tiers track existing residential segregation, producing geographically uneven connectivity no individual skill can overcome. (Not a term in the assigned readings; cite it to the literature, not to Robinson or Grimes.) | [Week 6](week-06-digital-divide/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Dispositions** | in competency frameworks, the attitudes a competent practitioner is expected to hold; Baer's target because dispositions statements encode feeling rules as professional requirements (Baer 2025). | [Week 16](week-16-futures/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Distributed data science fluency** | enough methodological literacy across all roles that projects can be championed *or challenged* from anywhere (p. 11). | [Week 11](week-11-automation/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Diversity vs. social justice** | Morales, Knowles, and Bourg's governing distinction: diversity describes a group's composition; social justice addresses power and privilege structurally, so a diverse staff running an unchanged system has achieved one and not the other. | [Week 8](week-08-social-justice/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Documentation movement** | Otlet and La Fontaine's project to apply scientific method and new retrieval technology to recorded information; the direct ancestor of information science. | [Week 1](week-01-disciplines/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Domain analysis** | Hjørland's approach: information problems studied within the knowledge domain and discourse community that produces them. | [Week 1](week-01-disciplines/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Dragnet enrollment** | Silently enrolling an entire described cohort without sampling, notice, or consent; Salo names Wollongong's Library Cube and the Minnesota cohort project. | [Week 5](week-05-data-ethics/concepts-and-questions.md) |

## F

| Term | Definition | Week |
|------|------------|------|

| **FAIR and CARE principles** | data-management principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) and Indigenous data governance principles (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) that IFLA asks libraries to respect; CARE also appears in Padilla. | [Week 12](week-12-makerspaces/concepts-and-questions.md) |

| **Field of cultural production** | Bourdieu's term, applied by Knox: the space where symbolic capital outranks economic capital. Libraries are anomalous because they collect both large-scale and restricted-scale production. | [Week 2](week-02-intellectual-freedom/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **Field of study** | Hirst's category, adopted by Bawden and Robinson: a domain organized around a topic rather than a unique form of knowledge. It explains why LIS lives in so many different faculties. | [Week 1](week-01-disciplines/concepts-and-questions.md) |
| **First sale (§ 109) and the § 108 exception** | The doctrines making lending and preservation copying possible for physical items, whose weakness in digital licensing is why the Manifesto's "reasonable terms" clause exists. | [Week 3](week-03-codes/concepts-and-questions.md) |
