# Week 7: Diversity, Equity, Inclusion & Belonging 🫱🏽‍🫲🏼

**Block 3: Diversity, Equity & Social Justice | October 5 – 11, 2026**

---

*The ALA added a word in 2025. Ask what the profession was admitting when it did.*

You are entering the part of the course where the profession's stated values and its actual demographics stop matching, and where you have to decide what to do about the gap. Everything you read this week circles one problem: libraries have declared themselves institutions of universal access for more than a century, and for most of that century the people staffing them, the collections they built, and the norms they enforced have reflected a narrow slice of the communities they claim to serve. The first two documents are the profession speaking in its official voice about how to close that gap. The last three argue that the official voice is part of the problem.

Start with the vocabulary, because it is contested and the contest matters. The ALA's interpretation was titled "Equity, Diversity, Inclusion" from its adoption in 2017 until May 29, 2025, when Council amended it to add *Belonging*. That is not decoration. Equity, diversity, and inclusion are things an institution does — allocates, counts, admits. Belonging is a state a person experiences, and it cannot be delivered by policy alone. Adding it commits ALA to a standard it cannot audit with a spreadsheet, which is either an advance in honesty or an invitation to unfalsifiable self-congratulation, depending on who you ask. Hold both possibilities. Notice too where the interpretation locates the source of these commitments: not in social responsibility but in intellectual freedom. Each section hangs on an article of the Library Bill of Rights. That is a deliberate and disputed move, and it is why the same document that demands libraries actively seek out marginalized voices also insists meeting rooms stay open to groups "some community members may find objectionable or offensive." Keep that seam in view; most live disagreements in American librarianship run along it, and Block 4 will pull on it hard.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Read the 2025 retitling of the EDI interpretation as an argument, not a rebrand
- Sit with Hathcock on whiteness as the profession's unmarked norm
- Learn the social model of disability through the new ALA ableism guidance
- Use interest convergence, intersectionality, and counter-storytelling as working tools

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- The ALA interpretation grounds equity in intellectual freedom rather than in social responsibility. What does the profession gain by that move, and what does it become unable to say?
- If "belonging" is something a person feels rather than something an institution provides, can a library be held accountable for producing it? What would evidence of failure look like?
- Hathcock argues that diversity programs recruit for whiteness. If so, is a better-designed program the remedy, or must the remedy be something other than a program?
- The ALA Ableism Q&A relocates accessibility from individual accommodation to structural obligation. Where else in this week's readings does that shift appear — or fail to?
