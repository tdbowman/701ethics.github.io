## 🗝️ Key Concepts

| Term | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Purpose / People / Pieces and Parts** | the three elements of the Making + Learning framework, in deliberate order, with tools chosen in service of purpose rather than purpose retrofitted to tools. |
| **Learning Practices of Making** | the seven observable behaviors from MAKESHOP: Inquire, Tinker, Seek and Share Resources, Hack and Repurpose, Express Intention, Develop Fluency, Simplify to Complexify; an attempt to make informal learning assessable. |
| **Text and data mining exception** | a statutory limitation permitting machine reading of lawfully accessed content; IFLA's core ask, and how jurisdictions without fair use reach LCA's result. |
| **Fair use for ingestion** | LCA's Principle 2, that building training databases from copyrighted works generally is fair use, with liability attaching at the output stage. |
| **De minimis contribution** | LCA's Principle 3 reasoning that any single work's contribution to an LLM is negligible, making per-work remuneration infeasible. |
| **FAIR and CARE principles** | data-management principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) and Indigenous data governance principles (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) that IFLA asks libraries to respect; CARE also appears in Padilla. |
| **Needs analysis vs. power analysis** | Marshall and Melo's distinction: asking what a community needs assumes that identifying needs brings equity; a power analysis examines funding, decision-making, policies, hiring, training, and the arrangement of the space (p. 86). |
| **AI-ready collections** | IFLA's framing of holdings prepared and licensed for computational use; the copyright-side counterpart to Padilla's machine-actionable collections. |
| **Staff discretion clause** | the provision routing the final decision on what may be made through staff: a general right to refuse (Maryland), prior approval (Syracuse), or consultation (Minnesota); where written policy becomes situated judgment. |
| **Prior restraint vs. service refusal** | the unresolved question of whether refusing to fabricate an object is censorship of expression or declination of a service; makerspace content rules turn on it. |
| **Copyright as a blunt instrument** | IFLA's warning that copyright should not be used to resolve ethical problems it was not designed for, which forces those problems back onto professional ethics. |

All terms from all sixteen weeks are collected in the [Glossary](../glossary.md).

## 💬 Annotation & Discussion Prompts

These are written for **Perusall**. Each one points at something specific and each one is arguable, which is the point: annotation credit comes from advancing a conversation, not from summarizing a paragraph.

1. Maryland refuses projects containing "symbols or words that can be deemed offensive or inappropriate by members of the campus community." Annotate that clause and argue whether it is compatible with the Library Bill of Rights. If you would keep it, write the sentence you would keep instead; if you would strike it, say what the staff member at the desk does on Monday.
2. Compare the weapons provisions at Minnesota ("actual functional weaponry"), Maryland ("look like, function as, or appear to function as"), and Syracuse ("weapon replicas of any kind"). Annotate whichever is best drafted and defend its line against the two adjacent positions.
3. LCA Principle 3 holds that per-work remuneration is "neither appropriate nor feasible." Annotate that sentence and separate the claims: is *infeasible* being used to establish *inappropriate*? Build the strongest response an author could make, then say whether a library association is the right body to be making this argument at all.
4. IFLA writes that copyright should not be a blunt force tool for ethical issues. Annotate that passage, identify one ethical problem with AI training data that copyright genuinely cannot solve, and name which instrument from Weeks 10–11 you would use instead.
5. **(Final-essay rehearsal)** The MAKESHOP Learning Practices define seven observable behaviors as evidence of learning. Annotate that table and write the case against it as an assessment instrument: what does it reward, what is invisible to it, what would a learner who understood the rubric do differently? Then apply your own critique to the Block 5 integrator's report about you, including the part that also indicts you.
6. The Making + Learning framework asks you to name your program's underlying values but proposes none. Take one of Marshall and Melo's four domains and run it on a makerspace you know, even from its website. Annotate the passage you used and say what it surfaced that "Why a makerspace?" would not. Then argue whether a values-neutral framework serves practitioners or launders inherited commitments.
7. Every policy examined routes final judgment through staff, by refusal, approval, or consultation. Annotate one such clause, ask Marshall and Melo's question of it (who enforces it, and by what process?), and decide: is discretion the humane residue that keeps policy from becoming machinery, or where bias operates without a record? Connect your answer to Mannheimer et al. on human-in-the-loop.

## 🔗 Connections

- **Scenario:** This week is the copyright negotiation point in the vendor role-play. LCA and IFLA give you two distinct positions to negotiate *from*, and knowing which you are using tells you what you can concede; ARL Principle 7, that licenses should not restrict scholarly use, is your redline. The codes of conduct supply the deliverable's format: a recommendation to deploy an AI service is not finished until you have drafted the two or three sentences of use policy that will hang beside it, including the discretion clause and who exercises it.
- **Final essay:** This week supplies the property and governance half of your critique. The MAKESHOP Learning Practices are the closest analogue in your readings to what the Block 5 integrator does to you: observable behaviors standing in for learning. Critiquing them is direct practice for Part 1. IFLA's "copyright should not be used as a blunt force tool" gives you a transferable move for Part 2: naming a problem the available instrument cannot solve is a stronger philosophical position than pretending it can, and it is exactly the shape of the "one unresolved tension" the essay requires. The codes of conduct model what an actionable commitment looks like written down: specific, enforceable, and costly to someone.
- **Threads forward:** Block 5's capstone asks you to integrate across all four blocks; Block 4 supplies the vocabulary for interrogating the instrument doing the integrating. Carry forward three things: provenance, validity, and discretion.

IFLA's earlier values-side statement (distinct from the 2025 copyright statement); the document Cox draws on for filter bubbles, forum moderation, deepfakes, and chilling effects.

The intergovernmental instrument behind much of the ALA guidance's ethics vocabulary; in the project as `UNESCORecommendationAI.pdf`. Useful for an argument made from outside librarianship.

- *In the Library with the Lead Pipe*. https://www.inthelibrarywiththeleadpipe.org. Open, peer-reviewed, and where critical practitioner arguments about library technology appear first; browse the AI tag alongside the journal's own published AI policy.
