# Course Wrap-Up 🎓

You started the semester by asking what profession this is. You are ending it by telling an algorithm what it got wrong about you. That arc was deliberate.

---

## What You Actually Did

- **Block 1** gave you the field's foundational documents and the first hard question: whose values does a library embody, and who decides?
- **Block 2** took the profession's most confident commitment, privacy, and showed you the vendor contracts, broadband maps, and patron preferences that complicate it.
- **Block 3** asked what a profession that calls itself neutral owes to the people its systems were not built for.
- **Block 4** put a technology in front of you that does not read your codes, and asked how you would govern it anyway.
- **Block 5** turned the question around and pointed it at you.

## What to Keep

Three things are worth carrying out of here:

1. **The habit of asking what is being measured.** It works on Pew tables, on vendor dashboards, on subject headings, and on the report that scored you.
2. **The distinction between a value and a commitment.** A value is what you would say in an interview. A commitment is what you would do on a Tuesday when it costs you something.
3. **Your unresolved tension.** You named one in the final essay. It will change shape over your career, and noticing when it does is what continuing professional development actually means.

## Where to Go Next

- Keep the [Glossary](glossary.md) and [References](references.md) — the reading list is a starting bibliography for any LIS paper you write from here.
- The IFLA and ALA documents you read are updated regularly. Re-read the ones that matter to your job every couple of years; they change more than people expect.
- Follow the open-access venues in the Going Deeper sections. *In the Library with the Lead Pipe*, *Journal of Radical Librarianship*, and the *International Review of Information Ethics* are free and will keep you current.
