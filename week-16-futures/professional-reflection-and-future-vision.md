# Week 16: Professional Reflection & Future Vision 🔭

**Block 5: Synthesis & Professional Integration | December 7 – 12, 2026**

---

*Before you write the essay, decide what you are allowed to feel about all this.*

Last week of the course, and your final essay is due Saturday, December 12. These readings do one job: give you material for a professional philosophy that is about the future without being naive about it.

Two are institutional futures documents — the IFLA Trend Report 2024 and the ACRL 2026 Top Trends in Academic Libraries — and one is an argument about why such documents so often cannot say anything difficult. Read Baer first; she will change how you read the other two. Baer does something unusual for professional literature: she takes the *emotional* dimension of professional discourse seriously and treats it as a site of power. Borrowing Arlie Hochschild's concept of feeling rules — social norms about which feelings are appropriate to have and display — she argues that library and higher-education discourse about generative AI has developed a set of them. Enthusiasm and optimism are appropriate; hesitancy is reframed as anxiety to be overcome; skepticism is coded as Luddism, obsolescence, holding the profession back. The effect is not to win the argument about AI but to prevent it from being had.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Name the feeling rules generative AI trains its users into
- Read trend reports as scenario tools rather than predictions
- Take refusal seriously as a professional stance, and know what it costs
- Assemble the philosophy you will argue for in the final essay

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- What feeling rules operate in your own workplace or classroom about technology, and what have you not said because of them?
- Both futures documents name AI as a defining trend. Does either leave room for a library to decline to adopt a technology, and what does the answer tell you about the genre?
- Across all five blocks: which reading changed your practice, as opposed to your opinion? What is the difference?
