# Week 3: Professional Codes of Ethics ⚖️

**Block 1: Foundations & Intellectual Freedom | September 7 – 13, 2026**

---

*Two codes, written for different worlds, that contradict each other in at least one place you will have to live in.*

This week you read three short documents that took a combined century and a half to write, and you will find they contradict each other in at least one important place. That contradiction is the assignment.

The IFLA Code, approved August 2012, states in Clause 5 that librarians "are strictly committed to neutrality and an unbiased stance regarding collection, access and service." The ALA Code of Ethics, in the ninth principle added June 29, 2021, commits members to "work to recognize and dismantle systemic and individual biases; to confront inequity and oppression; to enhance diversity and inclusion; and to advance racial and social justice." Knox told you last week that neutrality is not and never was an official ALA value. Read side by side, these documents write the profession's central unresolved argument into its own foundational texts, by two bodies sharing most of their membership.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Compare the IFLA and ALA codes clause by clause, including where they pull against each other
- Locate the neutrality clause and the social-responsibility principle, and hold both
- See what the 2025 copyright interpretation asks of you when a license overrides statute
- Leave with a position you can defend in the Censorship Crisis scenario

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- IFLA Clause 5 commits librarians to neutrality; ALA Principle 9 commits them to advancing racial and social justice. Is this a real contradiction, a difference in what neutrality is predicated of, or two defensible answers to a question with no single right answer?
- Principle 7 forbids letting personal beliefs interfere with fair representation of your institution's aims; Principle 9 requires confronting inequity and oppression. When your institution's aims are the inequity, which governs?
- IFLA says full compliance "is not expected"; ALA says its principles "cannot and do not dictate conduct." What authority does a code have if it disclaims its own enforceability?
