## 🗝️ Key Concepts

| Term | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Algorithmic bias** | biased outcomes arising from the algorithm or from historic data reflecting past inequalities, which AI reproduces (Cox, p. 205); ARL's Principle 2 frames it as "distortions" inherent to all AI. |
| **Managing bias vs. debiasing** | Mannheimer et al.'s conclusion that removing bias is unattainable and that transparency about known bias is the realistic commitment. |
| **Ethics washing** | publishing AI ethics principles as reputational response, motivated by a desire to avoid regulation (Cox, p. 206). |
| **Deresponsibilisation** | diffusion of accountability when a decision emerges from an opaque process rather than a human author (Cox, pp. 205–206). |
| **Explainability** | the demand that a system's decisions be intelligible and accountable; ARL ties it to Explainable AI and the "right to an explanation." |
| **Human-in-the-loop** | human intervention to support accuracy; the second most frequent "ethics" code in the literature, which Mannheimer et al. reclassify as technical. |
| **Chilling effect** | the climate in which patrons do not feel safe searching for or reading what they want (Cox, p. 208). |
| **Data sovereignty** | ALA's insistence that patron data, institutional data, local history, staff workflows, and usage logs remain under library, public-sector, or community control. |
| **Environmental cost of inference** | energy, emissions, water, e-waste, and extraction treated as ethics; explicit in ALA's Sustainability section and ACRL's Ethical Considerations. |
| **Unseen labor** | the annotation, moderation, and maintenance work that makes AI systems function (ACRL; Cox's "industrial complex"). |

All terms from all sixteen weeks are collected in the [Glossary](../glossary.md).

## 💬 Annotation & Discussion Prompts

These are written for **Perusall**. Each one points at something specific and each one is arguable, which is the point: annotation credit comes from advancing a conversation, not from summarizing a paragraph.

1. Annotate the ALA sentence prohibiting replacement of service roles with the strongest objection you can build from a library that cannot staff reference after 6 p.m. Then decide whether the sentence should be softened, and say what it would cost the profession if it were.
2. Mannheimer et al. remove accuracy and human-in-the-loop, dropping ethics coverage from 55% to 35%. Mark their justification. Is the reclassification defensible, or does it define ethics so as to guarantee the finding? Name the evidence that would change your mind.
3. Cox's scenario 6 wants a recommender explained and configured; ARL's Principle 4 wants a human at critical junctures. Annotate either and specify what "critical decision-making juncture" means for a system surfacing 20 titles per patron per day. Is a human in that loop at all?
4. **(Final-essay rehearsal)** Using ARL Principle 3 as your standard, annotate it with the three specific disclosures you would demand about the Block 5 integrator's report before accepting its judgment about you. Then say which of the three a library could realistically demand of a vendor, and why the gap matters.
5. ACRL names "centralized control of AI technologies and infrastructure" as a barrier to access; ARL Principle 1 says libraries democratize access to AI tools. Annotate one against the other. Can a library democratize access to a tool whose infrastructure three companies control, or is it distributing someone else's dependency?
6. Cox reports over-reliance on vendors to ensure a technology is ethical (p. 203). Find one place in the ALA guidance or ACRL competencies where that reliance is addressed and one where it is quietly assumed. Which document is more honest about the asymmetry?

## 🔗 Connections

- **Scenario:** This week supplies the evaluation apparatus for the chatbot branch and the bias simulations. Cox's scenarios 3 and 6 are near-exact rehearsals of the chatbot and recommender decisions; ACRL's Analysis and Evaluation competencies give you criteria to defend a recommendation; the ALA guidance's specific vendor disclosures — carbon estimates, data-center energy sources, water usage — turn "environmental cost" into a procurement demand in the cost-benefit branch.
- **Final essay:** These readings let you argue *against a judgment about yourself* rather than merely dislike it. Mannheimer et al. give you the move of asking what a system counts as ethics and showing its categories are technical ones in ethical clothing. Cox gives you validity: learning data "consists of what can be measured and is not often actually direct measurement of learning" (p. 208) — applicable to any score derived from your annotations. ARL Principle 3 gives you a disclosure standard; Principle 4 grounds for asking which human reviewed it. ALA's intellectual-freedom framing — systems threaten it when they obscure how information is ranked — generalizes cleanly to the ranking of you.
- **Threads forward:** Week 11 takes the labor question ALA opens and makes it the whole subject. Week 12 stress-tests ARL's Principle 6 against IFLA's competing copyright position.
