## 🗝️ Key Concepts

| Term | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Field of study** | Hirst's category, adopted by Bawden and Robinson: a domain organized around a topic rather than a unique form of knowledge. It explains why LIS lives in so many different faculties. |
| **Communication chain of recorded information** | Creation, dissemination, collection, storage, organisation, description, retrieval, use, preservation. Bawden and Robinson's claim to uniqueness rests on caring about the whole chain. |
| **Domain analysis** | Hjørland's approach: information problems studied within the knowledge domain and discourse community that produces them. |
| **Knowledge society** | The Manifesto's framing, in which libraries provide infrastructure for creating and sharing knowledge "without commercial, technological or legal barriers," not merely storing it. |
| **Data / information / knowledge** | Hepler and Horalek's ladder: bare facts, interpreted facts, internalized information. Useful as a teaching device, contested as a theory. |
| **The collection disciplines** | Bates's term for librarianship, archives, and museum documentation: the "disciplines of the cultural record," overlapping with but distinct from the information sciences. |
| **Documentation movement** | Otlet and La Fontaine's project to apply scientific method and new retrieval technology to recorded information; the direct ancestor of information science. |
| **Unique titles vs. challenge instances** | ALA counted 4,235 unique titles and 7,884 challenge instances in 2025. Conflating the two is the commonest error in public debate about these numbers. |

All terms from all sixteen weeks are collected in the [Glossary](../glossary.md).

## 💬 Annotation & Discussion Prompts

These are written for **Perusall**. Each one points at something specific and each one is arguable, which is the point: annotation credit comes from advancing a conversation, not from summarizing a paragraph.

1. Annotate the Manifesto's demand that copyright law give libraries "the same capacity to procure and give access to digital content on reasonable terms as is the case with physical resources." Is this a principle or a legislative demand? What would compliance cost, and who would bear it?
2. Bawden and Robinson rest disciplinary uniqueness on "the totality" of the communication chain. Annotate that passage and argue whether breadth can ground expertise. If an administrator asked what your degree qualifies you to do that a data scientist cannot, what is your answer?
3. Hepler and Horalek define information as interpreted data; Bawden and Robinson treat "information" as unsettled. Annotate either and take a side: is the simple model good enough for practice, or does the simplification hide the problems that matter?
4. Annotate ALA's 91.7% figure. What would you need to know about the methodology before citing it in public testimony, and does the number change what you say to a parent with a genuine concern?
5. The Manifesto requires preserving Indigenous and local knowledge "in accordance with the community's wishes" while asserting universal, uncensored access. Build the strongest case that these conflict, then resolve it.
6. Chapter 17 argues heritage-month programming marginalizes the communities it celebrates. Make the strongest case against that critique before deciding whether you accept it.

## 🔗 Connections

- **Scenario:** The 5C Framework opens with **Context**, and this week is your Context. The ALA report tells you what a challenge structurally looks like now, letting you ask the diagnostic question early: one patron or one campaign? The Manifesto gives you public language that is not merely your own opinion. Hepler and Horalek Ch. 3 tells you which staff and workflows a challenge will actually disrupt.
- **Final essay:** The communication chain lets you locate algorithmic systems precisely: recommendation and retrieval act on the *retrieval* and *use* links, built by people who own only that link. Bawden and Robinson's claim that information science alone attends to the totality is exactly the claim you can test against a system optimizing one segment in "Arguing with the Algorithm."
- **Threads forward:** Week 2 asks what the Manifesto's anti-censorship clause means in American law and ALA policy; Week 3 makes its implicit ethics explicit. Block 2 picks up its digital access and privacy commitments, Block 3 the equity critique in Ch. 17, Block 4 the communication chain under AI.
